Research

I work on simulations of magnetised plasmas, from turbulence in the solar wind to accretion discs around black holes.

Current sheets in decaying turbulence

PhD at Northumbria University, since March 2025

Turbulence in a collisionless plasma doesn't dissipate evenly. It concentrates into thin, intense current sheets, where the magnetic field changes direction sharply and magnetic energy is turned into heat and fast particles. I run large hybrid particle-in-cell simulations with the CAMELIA code, which treats ions as particles and electrons as a fluid, on DiRAC supercomputers. The aim is to understand the role these sheets play in decaying solar-wind turbulence, and to link their properties to magnetic reconnection, plasma heating, intermittency and the break in the turbulent spectrum at ion scales.

So far I've measured how current-sheet statistics depend on the ion and electron plasma beta, which I presented at NAM 2026 and the S³PARK workshop. At a DiRAC hackathon in 2026 I also ported key parts of CAMELIA to AMD GPUs.

Accretion discs in general relativity

Long-term visiting student at ICTS-TIFR, Bengaluru, since August 2024

With Prayush Kumar, I'm developing general-relativistic MHD simulations of a magnetised Fishbone–Moncrief accretion disc with the SpECTRE code, and checking their physical consistency and stability against the BHAC code. SpECTRE combines finite-difference and discontinuous Galerkin methods, which we're using to make simulations of relativistic flows faster, more accurate and more scalable.

Interests

High-energy astrophysical systems such as accretion discs, modelled with GRMHD and MHD simulations; machine learning for astrophysics, from image classification to simulations and gravitational-wave analysis; tests of modified gravity with the quasi-normal-mode frequencies of merging compact binaries; and turbulence in collisionless plasmas like the solar wind.
