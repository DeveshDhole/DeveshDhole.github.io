Research

The solar wind is a turbulent, almost collisionless plasma that spacecraft can measure from the inside, which makes it a natural laboratory for turbulence in space. My PhD combines kinetic simulations with those measurements to understand where turbulent energy goes at the smallest scales.

Finding current sheets

Turbulence in a collisionless plasma doesn't dissipate evenly. It concentrates into thin, intense current sheets, where magnetic energy is turned into heat and fast particles. I run hybrid particle-in-cell simulations with the CAMELIA code on the COSMA supercomputer in Durham, and compare methods for detecting these sheets automatically and measuring how thick they are.

One question I'm working on is how their properties change with plasma beta, the ratio of thermal to magnetic pressure.

Virtual spacecraft

A spacecraft records a single line through the plasma as it streams past. To compare simulations with Parker Solar Probe, Solar Orbiter and MMS on equal terms, I fly virtual spacecraft through simulation boxes and analyse the resulting time series with the same tools used on real data. The simulation on the home page is a small version of the idea.
